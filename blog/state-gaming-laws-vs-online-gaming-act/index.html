Does Your State's Old Online Gaming Law Still Apply in 2026?

Published: 24 November 2026 · Last Reviewed: 24 November 2026 · By the All Yono India editorial team · How we check facts

Short answer: no, not on its own. Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Odisha, Nagaland, and Sikkim each had their own position on online gaming before 2025 — some prohibiting it outright, others licensing it at the state level. Since 1 May 2026, the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 prohibits online money games nationwide, regardless of what any individual state previously allowed, licensed, or banned. A state's older position no longer decides the outcome for an online money game by itself.

Why the National Act Overrides These Older Approaches

The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, together with the Online Gaming Rules, 2026, has applied across India since 1 May 2026. Section 5 prohibits offering an online money game, and Section 9(1) sets a penalty of up to three years in prison, a fine of up to ₹1 crore, or both — the same national framework described in our guide to the Act. Nothing in the Act carves out an exception for a state that previously licensed or permitted a money-based online format: a state license or permission issued before 1 May 2026 does not override a central Act that came into force after it.

This cuts both ways. A state that had already banned online money gaming, like Telangana or Andhra Pradesh, now has the national law reinforcing that position. A state that had instead built a licensing regime, like Nagaland or Sikkim, now has the national law applying over the top of that regime for anything that qualifies as an online money game.

Do Nagaland's and Sikkim's Licenses Still Work?

Not for online money games. Both states built a licensing structure aimed at regulating specified online "games of skill" at the state level, years before the national Act existed. A license granted under either framework addressed that state's own rules at the time — it did not, and could not, anticipate a central law passed afterward. If a platform points to a Nagaland or Sikkim license as proof that its current real-money offering is legal nationwide, that claim does not address the Act described above. We have not conducted a platform-specific review of any individual licensed operator, and a pre-2025 state license is not independent confirmation of current compliance.

Does This Change Anything for Offline, In-Person Play?

No. The national Act covers online games specifically — offline, in-person play sits outside its scope, and each state's own law continues to govern it exactly as it did before 1 May 2026. If you're trying to work out the status of an in-person game rather than an app or website, the relevant law is still your state's, not the national Act.

What This Means If You're Searching "[Your State] Online Gaming Law"

Most pages written around a specific state's online gaming position predate the 2025 Act and describe a world that no longer exists on its own terms. Before relying on one:

  • Check whether the page has a visible publish or review date — anything from before August 2025 is describing the pre-Act landscape only.
  • Treat a platform's claim of being "licensed in [state]" as a historical fact about that state's older framework, not as proof of current nationwide legality.
  • Remember that your state's older position and the national Act can point the same way (most states that restricted gaming still do, now more strongly) or can conflict (a state licensing regime does not survive contact with a later prohibition on money games).
  • Use the Disclaimer for the site's jurisdiction notice, and speak with a qualified legal professional in your state for advice about your specific situation.

FAQs

Does my state's old online gaming law still apply in 2026?

Not on its own. Since 1 May 2026, the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 prohibits online money games across India regardless of what any individual state previously banned, licensed, or allowed. A state's older position is no longer the deciding factor for an online money game.

Which states had their own online gaming restrictions before the national Act?

Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Odisha, Nagaland and Sikkim each had pre-existing state-level approaches to online gaming before the 2025 Act — some prohibiting online money formats, others operating a state licensing framework for specified online games. The exact statutory text differs by state; check the relevant state gazette for specifics.

Is online rummy legal in Telangana now?

Telangana had already amended its state gaming law to restrict online money gaming before the national Act existed. Since 1 May 2026, the national Act applies on top of that, prohibiting online rummy for money across India — so Telangana's own pre-2025 position and the national position point the same direction for online money rummy.

Do Nagaland's or Sikkim's online gaming licenses still work?

Nagaland and Sikkim each operated a state licensing framework for specified online games before the national Act. A license issued under either state framework before 1 May 2026 does not create an exemption from a central Act that prohibits online money games nationwide. We have not reviewed any individual licensed operator's current status, and a platform citing an old state license is not independent confirmation of current legality.

Does this affect offline, in-person gaming in these states?

No. The national Act covers online games specifically. Offline, in-person play is outside the Act's scope, and each state's own law continues to apply to it exactly as before.
