Last Reviewed: 26 June 2026

This page is a quick-reference companion to our longer explainer, Is Online Rummy Legal in India? 2026 Rules Explained. It summarises general state-level posture in one place — but it cannot replace checking your own state's current law.

This page is general information only and is not legal advice. Laws change, and state-level positions can be updated, challenged in court, or reinterpreted at any time. Always verify current law for your specific location.

Recap: Why This Is More Than a Skill-vs-Chance Question

Rummy has long been treated by Indian courts as predominantly a game of skill, following rulings such as State of Andhra Pradesh v. K. Satyanarayana (1968). However, a 2026 national framework for online money games shifted the more important question to whether money or a stake is involved — not just whether skill is present. FAQs

Is this page a complete list of every state law on rummy?

No. It is a general quick-reference summary, not a complete or current legal record. Always verify your own state's current law.

Does a state not listed here mean online rummy is fully permitted there?

Not necessarily. Even without a specific state-level ban, the 2026 national framework for online money games can still apply.

Why do Nagaland and Sikkim appear different from the other listed states?

Those two operate licensing regimes in some categories rather than a blanket ban, which is a different regulatory approach from outright restriction.

Can a state's legal position change after this article is published?

Yes. State laws, court rulings, and national frameworks can all change. Check current official sources rather than relying solely on this page.

Is rummy's "game of skill" status still relevant?

It remains part of the legal background, but it does not by itself determine whether an online, stake-based format is currently permitted. See our full rummy-law explainer for more detail.
