All Yono App Permissions Explained: What's Normal and What's a Red Flag

Last Reviewed: 22 June 2026

Many users only think about app permissions after they have already installed something — usually when a pop-up asks for access to contacts, SMS, or the camera. This page explains which permission requests are typical for the type of apps listed in the All Yono directory, and which ones deserve a second look before you tap "Allow."

All Yono India does not request any permission, login detail, or personal information through this website. Everything described below relates to permissions an app may request on your device after installation — not anything this website asks for.

Why App Permissions Matter

An Android permission controls what part of your phone an app is allowed to access — storage, camera, contacts, SMS, location, and more. Some permissions are reasonable for a given app's purpose. Others are not, and a mismatch between what an app does and what it asks for is one of the clearest warning signs available to an everyday user.

Reviewing permissions takes less than a minute and does not require technical knowledge — just a willingness to pause before accepting.

Permissions That Are Often Expected

For the type of mobile game or app listed in this directory, a few permission requests are common and generally tied to a clear purpose:

  • Storage access — to save game data, cache, or downloaded content on your device.
  • Network access — required for any app that connects to a server, which applies to nearly all listed apps.
  • Notifications — used to alert you about in-app updates or events, if you allow it.

These are not guarantees that a specific app is safe — they are simply requests that have an obvious, explainable purpose.

Permissions That Deserve a Closer Look

Some permissions are harder to justify for a game or card-app style platform. That does not automatically mean an app is harmful, but it does mean you should pause and ask why the permission is needed before continuing:

  • SMS access — rarely necessary for a game; be cautious if it is requested without a clear, stated reason.
  • Contacts access — only relevant if a feature explicitly depends on it, such as an optional referral tool.
  • Camera or microphone access — has no obvious purpose for most card, rummy, slots, or arcade-style apps.
  • Call logs or phone state — rarely needed outside of specific account-verification features.
  • Accessibility service access — this is one of the most powerful permissions on Android and should never be granted without clearly understanding why an app needs it.

If a permission request does not match what the app is supposed to do, the safer choice is to deny it or stop the installation rather than guess.

How to Review Permissions After Installing

On most Android devices, you can review and change app permissions through Settings → Apps → [app name] → Permissions. From there you can see exactly what access has been granted and revoke anything that does not seem necessary.

It is a good habit to check this screen shortly after installing any app — not just the ones listed on this site.

If a permission request feels unrelated to the app's purpose, you are not required to accept it to "see what happens." It is reasonable to deny the request, close the app, or uninstall it entirely if the behaviour continues to feel unclear.

FAQs

Does All Yono India ask for permissions on this website?

No. All Yono India is an informational website and does not request permissions, logins, OTPs, or personal details. Permission requests described in this article come from separate third-party apps after installation.

Is it normal for a rummy or slots app to request storage access?

Yes, storage access is commonly used to save game data or cached content and is generally considered a reasonable request for this type of app.

Why would a game app ask for SMS or contacts access?

There is rarely a clear reason for a card or slots-style app to need SMS or contacts access. If this is requested without an explained purpose, treat it as a reason to pause and review the request carefully.

What is accessibility service access, and why is it risky?

Accessibility service access allows an app to read and interact with content on your screen. It is intended for assistive technology, and granting it to an unrelated app can give that app far more control than a typical game should need.

Can I change app permissions after installing?

Yes. On most Android devices you can review and revoke permissions through Settings, Apps, the specific app, and then Permissions.
