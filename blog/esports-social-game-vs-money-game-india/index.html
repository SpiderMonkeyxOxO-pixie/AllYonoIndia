Esports and Social Games vs Online Money Games: What India's Act Exempts

Published: 28 November 2026 · Last Reviewed: 28 November 2026 · By the All Yono India editorial team · How we check facts

Short answer: the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 does not draw one line between "banned" and "allowed." Based on cross-checked secondary legal commentary, it sorts every online game into one of three boxes: an online money game (banned outright under Sections 5–7), an e-sport (legal, and registerable with the Online Gaming Authority of India once recognised under the National Sports Governance Act, 2025), or an online social game (legal, can charge a subscription or access fee, cannot take stakes). The box a game falls into turns on one question — does a user pay money or stakes in expectation of winning money or other enrichment back — not on whether the game involves skill. That single distinction is also why real-money rummy, and the apps this site covers, don't get to call themselves esports just because Indian courts have called rummy a game of skill in other contexts.

One claim in this piece (that online money games are explicitly barred from esport recognition) appears in only one of the outlets we checked and has not been independently re-verified against the primary Rules text; it is flagged below and in the FAQ.

Three Categories, Not One Ban

Most coverage of the Online Gaming Act focuses on what it prohibits. Less discussed is that the Act's own definitions section carves out two categories of game the ban doesn't touch at all. According to PRS Legislative Research's official Bill summary and corroborating commentary from Nishith Desai Associates and Bar & Bench, the Act defines three distinct categories: an online money game is one where a user pays money or other stakes — including credits, coins, or tokens that are equivalent or convertible to money — in expectation of winning money or other enrichment, regardless of whether the outcome depends on skill, chance, or both. An e-sport is an online game played in organised, multiplayer, pre-defined-rules competition, recognised under the National Sports Governance Act, 2025, where the outcome depends solely on skills like physical dexterity, mental agility, or strategy; it may charge entry or registration fees and award prize money, but not bets, wagers, or other stakes. An online social game is one offered solely for recreation, entertainment, or skill development, which may charge a subscription or one-time access fee but cannot involve stakes or an expectation of monetary gain in return for them.

Why "It's a Skill Game" Doesn't Move an App Out of the Banned Category

Before this Act, India's gambling law tradition drew its main line between games of skill and games of chance, and courts including the Supreme Court have at various points treated rummy as predominantly a game of skill. That history is part of why some operators and commentators expected a skill-based carve-out here too. Based on the definitions reported above, the 2025 Act doesn't use that test. The online money game definition applies "regardless of whether the outcome of such game is based on skill, chance, or both" — so a game can be entirely skill-based and still land in the banned category, if money or stakes change hands with an expectation of winning more back. Multiple legal commentators we checked flag this as a deliberate, and contested, departure from the skill-versus-chance line: critics argue it overrides the skill-game precedent rather than codifying it. Whatever the merits of that debate, the practical effect is that a real-money rummy, poker, or card app doesn't get to rely on "it's a skill game, not gambling" as a way out of the online-money-game category under this Act, because the category was written around stakes and expected winnings, not around skill.

Can an App Just Register as an Esport Instead?

This is the question we most wanted a primary-source answer to, and couldn't get one today. One trade-press report on the April 2026 Rules notification (PocketGamer.biz) states that online money games are explicitly barred from recognition as esports under the National Sports Governance Act, 2025 — meaning a game already classified as an online money game cannot separately qualify for esport status no matter how it's marketed. A second report on the same Rules (The Tribune) doesn't use that specific wording, but describes a consistent mechanism: the Online Gaming Authority of India determines whether a game is an online money game based on factors like player fees, expected monetary wins, and whether in-game assets trade for value outside the platform, and games classified as money games face "a distinct and stringent set of regulations" rather than the standard esport or social-game registration path. Both reports point the same direction — classification as a money game and registration as an esport are not meant to be interchangeable labels an operator can pick — but only one names an explicit statutory bar, and we have not independently confirmed that specific wording against the Rules themselves. Treat it as well-sourced trade reporting, not a verbatim quote of the law.

What Changed on 1 May 2026

The Act received presidential assent in August 2025, but the mechanics for sorting games into these three categories didn't exist until the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Rules, 2026 were notified by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology in April 2026 and took effect on 1 May 2026. Those Rules, per the same trade-press reporting, established the Online Gaming Authority of India as the body that recognises, categorises, and registers online games. It runs a reported 90-day determination process to classify a given title as either a prohibited online money game or a permissible esport or social game. Esport titles that register receive a digital certificate valid for up to 10 years, which registered platforms must display to users. The Centre can also require registration for specific categories of online social games, particularly ones posing risks to children or involving high transaction volumes, though which categories have actually been notified for that requirement is not something we could confirm as of this writing.

What This Means If You're Looking at a Real-Money Rummy or Card App

If an app takes an entry fee or stake and pays out winnings based on game results, the category question above resolves the same way regardless of how the app brands itself: it is the profile of an online money game under the Act's own stakes-and-expected-return test, and the prohibitions on offering, advertising, and payment facilitation described in our player-vs-operator penalty explainer apply to it. A label like "skill game," "esport mode," or "social tournament" attached to that same stakes mechanic doesn't appear, on this reading, to change which category it falls into. That's a separate question from whether a player can be penalised for using such an app (covered in that linked piece) and from what happens to money already sitting in one (covered in our stuck-balance explainer). This article only answers the categorisation question: which box a game sits in, and why skill alone or a rebrand alone doesn't move it.

FAQs

Does India's Online Gaming Act ban every real-money or skill-based game?

No. Based on cross-checked secondary legal commentary on the Act, it splits games into three categories: online money game (banned), e-sport (recognised and registerable), and online social game (recognised and registerable). Only the first category is prohibited; the other two are not, provided they meet the no-stakes conditions described below.

Can a real-money rummy or card app just register itself as an esport to stay legal?

Based on April 2026 trade-press reporting on the notified Rules, no: online money games are reported as explicitly barred from esport recognition under the National Sports Governance Act, 2025, which esport status requires. A game that charges stakes with an expectation of monetary return stays classified as an online money game regardless of what it calls itself, as far as this single-sourced report goes; we have not independently re-verified this specific exclusion against the primary Rules text.

What is the legal difference between an "online social game" and an "online money game"?

Based on this reading of secondary commentary, an online social game may charge a subscription or one-time access fee but cannot involve stakes or an expectation of monetary gain in return; an online money game involves paying money or stakes specifically in expectation of winning money or other enrichment, regardless of whether the game depends on skill, chance, or both.

Has India actually started registering esports and social games?

Based on April 2026 reporting, the Online Gaming Authority of India was established once the Rules took effect on 1 May 2026, with a 90-day determination process to classify games and esport registration certificates valid for up to 10 years. We have not independently verified how many titles have been registered as of this writing.

Where can I read the primary source myself?

The Act is published as Act No. 32 of 2025 on indiacode.nic.in, and the 2026 Rules were announced via a Press Information Bureau release; both government links returned an error on our most recent fetch attempt, so we relied on secondary legal and trade-press commentary instead.
